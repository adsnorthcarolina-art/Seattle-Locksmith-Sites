Every kind of car key

  • Transponder (chip) keys. The standard key on most cars since the late 1990s. Cut and programmed to the immobilizer on site.
  • Remote-head keys. Key and remote in one unit. Lock/unlock buttons programmed along with the chip.
  • Smart keys and push-to-start fobs. Proximity fobs for keyless-entry and keyless-start cars, including many 2015+ models.
  • Laser-cut (high-security) keys. The thick sidewinder keys used by Honda, Toyota, Lexus, BMW, Mercedes, VW and others.
  • Standard metal keys. Older cars, trucks and work vehicles, cut by code.
  • Spare keys and duplicates. Cheaper than a replacement after you've lost the last one — most {{city}} customers ask for a spare while we're there.
  • Key fob battery and shell replacement. Cracked case or worn buttons, swapped without reprogramming.

All keys lost? Not a problem.

You don't need an existing key. The technician reads the key code from the car's VIN or the door lock, cuts the blank, and programs it to the immobilizer with dealer-level tools. You drive away with a working key, the old lost key is erased from the car, and nobody who finds it can start your car.
