You've got the keys to your new place in {{city}}. So, probably, do the previous owner's cleaner, dog walker, contractor and at least one ex. The fix is quick and cheap — but it helps to know which fix you're paying for.

Rekeying

The locksmith opens each lock cylinder, swaps the small pins inside, and cuts a new key. The old keys stop working, the new key works on every door, and the hardware on the door doesn't change. It takes a few minutes per lock. If your locks are a decent brand in good condition, this is the answer.

Replacing

Pull the lock, fit a new one. Costs more because you're buying hardware. Worth it when the existing lock is worn, loose, a cheap builder-grade unit, or when you want something the old lock can't do — a smart lock, a high-security cylinder, a proper deadbolt where there was only a knob.

What we usually recommend

  • Rekey everything to one key the day you move in.
  • Replace any lock that's loose, sticky or visibly cheap.
  • Add a deadbolt to any exterior door that doesn't have one.
  • Don't forget the garage entry door and the sliding patio door.
